Project Summary

This project applied untargeted metabolomics technology to conduct research. The instrument platforms used were Q Exactive. A total of 1698 positive ion-mode metabolites and 1266 negative ion-mode metabolites were identified from the 28 samples in this project. Differential metabolites were screened according to the criteria of VIP > 1.0, FC > 1.2 or FC < 0.833 and P-value < 0.05. For example, there were 101 positive ion-mode metabolites with significant differences in the Group1.vs.Group2 comparison group, of which 59 metabolites were up-regulated and 42 metabolites were down-regulated. There were 44 negative ion-mode metabolites with significant differences, of which 21 metabolites were up-regulated and 23 metabolites were down-regulated. Based on the above data, systematic pathway annotation was performed for all identified metabolites, and differential analysis and KEGG analyses were performed for all differential metabolites. The specific workflow and analysis are as follows.

Research Overview

Metabolome refers to the collection of all small molecular metabolites (mainly endogenous small molecules with a relative molecular weight less than 1000 Da) in cells, tissues, organs or organisms.

Metabolomics is a new technology emerging after genomics, transcriptomics and proteomics. It is an important part of systems biology. Metabolomics aims to investigate the changes of metabolic products or the changes with time after the cells and tissues of organisms are stimulated or disturbed. Through screening the differential metabolites of the experimental group and the control group, the biological processes involved by the differential metabolites are studied, and the mechanism of their participation in life activities is revealed.

Metabolomics is more similar to phenotypes. It is an extension of transcriptomics and proteomics, and can reflect the physiological state of organisms more directly and accurately. Bill Lasley and UC Davis once pointed out that "genomics and proteomics tell you what may happen, while metabolomics tell you what has actually happened".

Untargeted metabolomics aims at discovery. It is necessary to detect as many metabolites as possible in biological samples and reflect the total metabolite information to the greatest extent. Therefore, untargeted metabolomics must equip with high-resolution mass spectrometer. The higher the resolution, the more metabolites detected by the mass spectrometer. The combination of ultra-high performance liquid chromatography (UHPLC) and high resolution mass spectrometer (HRMS), namely, LC-MS/MS, has been widely applied in untargeted metabolomics research due to its advantages of wide range of detected substances and simple sample pretreatment.

Project Workflow

1. Experiment workflow

This project is based on the liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry (LC-MS) technology [1-2] to conduct untargeted metabolomics research. The experiment workflow mainly includes: sample metabolite extraction, LC-MS/MS detection and data analysis, etc. Due to the characteristics of metabolomics, such as rapid changes in metabolites, a wide variety of metabolites, large differences in concentrations, diverse chemical properties, and huge data information, every step from sample collection, storage, metabolite extraction, and mass spectrometry detection may have an impact on the quality of data. The quality of data will directly affect the results of subsequent information analysis. In order to ensure the accuracy and reliability of the data from the source, Novegene meticulously controls every step of the experiment and realizes the standardized operation of metabolomics, fundamentally ensuring the production of high-quality data. 2. Bioinformatics analysis workflow

First, the raw mass spectrometry data was converted to mzXML format using ProteoWizard [3] . Then, peak extraction, alignment, and retention time correction were performed with XCMS software [4]. Subsequently, the total peak area within each sample was normalized, and peaks with a missing rate greater than 50% across sample groups were filtered out. The corrected and filtered results were matched with the Novogene local database to obtain metabolite identification information. Following this, multivariate statistical analysis was conducted on the metabolites, including Principal Component Analysis (PCA) and Partial Least Squares Discriminant Analysis (PLS-DA), to reveal metabolic pattern differences between groups. Hierarchical Clustering Analysis (HCA) and metabolite correlation analysis were performed to uncover relationships between samples and among metabolites. Functional analyses, such as metabolic pathway analysis, were conducted to interpret the biological significance associated with metabolites. 2. Qualitative and quantitative analysis of metabolites

2.1 Data pretreatment and metabolite identification

Based on high-resolution mass spectrometry (HRMS) detection technology, combined with high-quality secondary spectrum databases for matching and identification of molecular characteristic peaks, achieves high-throughput and high-quality metabolite identification in biological samples, reflecting the information of total metabolites to the maximum extent.

The raw data from the mass spectrometer is pre-processed using XCMS software. First, peak extraction, peak quantification, and peak alignment were performed. Then, based on the specified ppm and adduct information, the data is compared with a high-quality MS/MS spectral database for metabolite identification. Finally, metabolites with a coefficient of variance (CV) below 30% [5] in QC samples were retained as the final identification results for subsequent analysis.

3. Data quality control

The metabolomics is easy to be disturbed by external factors and changes rapidly. Therefore, data quality control (QC) is a necessary step to obtain stable and accurate metabolomics results. Especially when the sample size is large, it takes a certain time for the sample to be tested on the instrument. It is particularly important to check whether the stability of the instrument and the signal response strength are normal in the process of metabolite detection. The quality control system can timely detect abnormalities and solve problems as soon as possible to ensure the quality of the final collected data.

3.1 Correlation analysis of QC samples

Pearson correlation coefficient between QC samples is calculated based on the relative quantitative value of metabolites [6]. The higher the correlation of QC samples (|r| is closer to 1), the better the stability of the whole detection process and the higher the data quality. 3.2 PCA analysis of total samples

Principal component analysis (PCA) is a statistical method that converts a group of variables that may have correlation into a group of linear uncorrelated variables through orthogonal transformation. The converted group of variables is called principal component. Metabolomic data can be considered as a multivariate data set. PCA can reduce the dimension of metabolite variables by linear combination according to a certain weight, and then generate new characteristic variables, which are classified by the similarity of the main new variables (principal components), reflecting the overall metabolic difference between each group of samples and the degree of variation between samples within the group. MetaX [7] software is used for logarithmic conversion and standardization of data.

The peaks extracted from all experimental samples and QC samples were analyzed by PCA analysis. The smaller the difference of QC samples, the better the stability of the whole method, the higher the data quality, which is reflected in the PCA analysis chart, that is, the distribution of QC samples will gather together. On the basis of preserving the original information to the greatest extent, the collected multidimensional data should be analyzed by dimensionality reduction and regression, and then the differential metabolites should be screened and subsequently analyzed.

5.2 Partial least squares discrimination analysis (PLS-DA)

PLS-DA is a supervised discriminant analysis statistical method. This method uses partial least squares regression [8] to establish the relationship model between the relative quantitative value of metabolites and the sample category to realize the prediction of the sample category. The PLS-DA model of each comparison group was established, and the model evaluation parameters (R2, Q2) obtained by 7-cycle cross-validation (seven cycle cross-validation; when the sample biological repetition number n ≤ 3, it is k cycle cross-validation, k=2n), if R2 and Q2 are closer to 1, it indicates that the model is more stable and reliable.

In order to judge the quality of the model, the model will also be sorted and verified to check whether the model is "over-fitting". Whether the model is "over fitting" reflects whether the model is constructed accurately. Not "over fitting" indicates that the model can better describe the sample and can be used as the premise for the model biomarker group search. "over fitting" indicates that the model is not suitable for describing the sample, and is not suitable for later analysis based on this data. The specific method [9] is to randomly mix the grouping marks of each sample before modeling and prediction. Each modeling corresponds to a set of R2 and Q2 values. Their regression lines can be obtained from the Q2 and R2 values after 200 times of mixing and modeling. When R2 data is greater than Q2 data and the intercept between Q2 regression line and Y-axis is less than 0, it can indicate that the model is not "over-fitting". 6.4 Cluster analysis of differential metabolites

Cluster analysis is used to determine the metabolic pattern of metabolites under different experimental conditions. Metabolites with similar metabolic patterns may have similar functions or participate in the same metabolic process or cell pathway. Therefore, the function of some metabolites can be inferred by grouping metabolites with the same or similar metabolic patterns.

7.5 KEGG control network diagram

In the field of biochemistry, metabolic pathway refers to a series of biochemical reactions in the process of transforming metabolic substances in cells into new metabolic substances under the action of enzymes. The metabolic network refers to the network that describes the metabolism and physiological processes in cells, which is composed of metabolic reactions and regulatory mechanisms that regulate these reactions. Based on the differential metabolites obtained from the previous analysis, a network-based enrichment analysis is constructed. By default, the KEGG control network diagram uses the Diffusion algorithm for enrichment. The principle behind it is to use the finite difference formula of the heat conduction equation to spread the heat marker from the metabolites to the rest of the node network. This method enables the metabolites of the input network to have thermal markers, which will spread to all the path nodes in the enrichment network. These nodes include the input metabolites, and also include information such as reactions, enzymes, modules and pathways. Finally, the network connected by nodes can be displayed by drawing, which can reflect the intersection between metabolic pathways and target potential enzymes and metabolites under specific research conditions, and show how the disturbance spreads at the level of the pathway and how the pathways interact with each other, thus enhancing the interpretability of the results [24-28]. 8　GSEA analysis

Traditional KEGG enrichment methods are based on the hypergeometric test for differentially expressed metabolites, but when the expression of a single metabolite does not change much, the results obtained based on traditional enrichment analysis may be little or even no results. GSEA (Gene Set Enrichment Analysis) [29-30] can effectively compensate for the lack of data mining in traditional enrichment analysis, and more comprehensively explain the regulatory role of a functional unit (pathway, GO term, or others). The basic idea is to use a predefined set of metabolites, rank the metabolites according to the degree of differential expression in two types of samples, and then test whether the predefined set of metabolites is enriched at the top or at the end of the ranked list.

9 ROC curve Analysis of differential metabolite

ROC curve (Receiver operating characteristic) [31], also known as the subject working characteristic curve or sensitivity curve, is a curve drawn according to a series of different dichotomous methods (boundary value or determination threshold), with the true positive rate (sensitivity) as the ordinate and the false positive rate (1-specificity) as the abscissa. The obtained differential metabolites were used to evaluate the potential biomarkers by ROC curve.

The area under the ROC curve is called Area Under Curve (AUC). AUC is used to evaluate the sensitivity and specificity of biomarkers to predict the occurrence of events. The sensitivity and specificity of each metabolite is determined by the optimal threshold of the ROC curve. When AUC value = 0.5, this biomarker has no effect on predicting the occurrence of events and has no predictive value. When the AUC value is greater than 0.5, the closer the AUC value is to 1, the higher the prediction accuracy is. Generally speaking, the prediction accuracy is low when the AUC value is between 0.5 and 0.7, there is a certain prediction accuracy when the AUC value is between 0.7 and 0.9, and there is a high prediction accuracy when the AUC value is above 0.9.
