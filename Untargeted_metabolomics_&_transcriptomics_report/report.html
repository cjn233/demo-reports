Research Overview

The rapid development of multi-omics technologies has facilitated a transition from detailed reductionist studies to systematic, integrated investigations, enabling different omics datasets to complement and validate each other for comprehensive functional genomic interpretation. Genomics reveals the relationship between gene structure and function through whole-genome sequencing and functional annotation; transcriptomics studies gene transcription profiles and regulatory mechanisms at the whole-cell level; proteomics investigates the composition and dynamic changes of proteins as direct functional executors in organisms; while metabolomics examines the temporal changes of metabolites in biological systems under external stimuli or perturbations, representing the discipline closest to phenotypic manifestations. This integrated multi-omics approach provides a powerful framework for understanding biological systems from molecular to functional levels.[1]The integration of multi-omics data enables a comprehensive understanding of biological systems, and as research at individual omics layers progressively matures, the transition from compartmentalized investigations to holistic system-level analysis emerges as an inevitable trajectory in scientific advancement.

Transcriptomics investigates gene expression patterns at the RNA level, serving as a crucial approach for studying cellular functions.[2]Metabolomics, as an extension of it, is to reveal the mechanisms underlying biological processes by analyzing systematic changes in small-molecule metabolites. Metabolomics provides more phenotypically significant data support for transcriptomics. Our aim in integrating them is:

(1) Cellular activities are coordinately regulated by genes, proteins, and small-molecule metabolites, where functional changes in macromolecules ultimately manifest at the metabolic level. Subtle alterations in gene expression become amplified in metabolic profiles, thereby enhancing detection sensitivity;

(2) Non-functional genetic variations are not reflected in metabolic profiles, effectively filtering out noise during information flow from upstream to downstream pathwaysNon-functional genetic variations are not reflected in metabolic profiles, effectively filtering out noise during information flow from upstream to downstream pathways;

(3) The number of metabolite species is substantially smaller than that of genes.

Comparative analysis between transcriptomic and metabolomic data can uncover post-transcriptional regulatory states of gene expression. Consequently, the relationship between transcriptomics and metabolomics will likely become an indispensable component of future systems biology research. 1.4 Nine-Quadrant correlation analysis

Quadrant analysis is a widely used method in correlation studies that enables deeper understanding and insights from complex datasets. The nine-quadrant approach allows identification of key factors with the greatest influence on specific biological phenomena, reveals potential relationships between different variables, and provides visual representation through partitioning data into nine distinct quadrants. In multi-omics integration studies, this method effectively analyzes correlations between two omics data types. The metabolite-gene nine-quadrant plot specifically facilitates screening of significantly altered metabolite-gene pairs, where the abscissa represents transcriptomic fold changes (log2-transformed) and the ordinate displays metabolomic fold changes (log2-transformed). The results are shown in Figure 5.4 (see the result file for the high-definition version of the picture).

2.Correlation analysis of differential gene and metabolite expression

2.1 Correlation heatmap analysis

To evaluate the correlation between significantly differential genes identified through transcriptomics analysis and differential metabolites derived from metabolomics analysis by calculating Pearson correlation coefficient between genes and metabolites. Gene-metabolite pairs with correlation coefficients |r| less than 0 were defined as exhibiting negative correlation, while those with |r| greater than 0 demonstrated positive correlation.

Figure2.1 Differential metabolites and genes expression correlation analysis heatmap (The upper box is the positive ion mode, the lower box is the negative ion mode)

Note: The vertical axis represents differential metabolites , while the horizontal axis displays differential genes . The blue represents negative correlation, and the red represents positive correlation. The presented correlation table features the top 500 most statistically significant differential metabolites and genes (ranked by ascending p-value), demonstrating pairwise interactions between these prioritized molecular features.

The metabolites were marked by yellow and the genes were marked by blue, the line between the metabolites and genes represents the correlation between the two metabolites. The results are shown below:

Figure2.2 Correlation network diagram (The upper box is the positive ion mode, the lower box is the negative ion mode)

Note：In the graphical representation, rectangular nodes denote molecular entities, with color coding distinguishing metabolite and gene features: yellow rectangles represent metabolites while blue rectangles indicate genes. Edge coloration encodes correlation directionality, with red lines designating positive associations (r > 0) and blue lines signifying negative relationships (r < 0). Edge color intensity scales proportionally with the absolute correlation coefficient magnitude (|r|), where deeper hues reflect stronger correlations.

3.O2PLS data integration analysis

The O2PLS (Two-Way Orthogonal Partial Least Squares) approach enables bidirectional modeling and predictive analysis between two omics datasets, facilitating the identification of intrinsic relationships and quantification of inter-omics associations. The two-omics data inputted to O2PLS must be potentially associated, and the results of the analysis show the differential genes and differential metabolites related to the association between the two-omics and the genes and metabolites that rank in the top 10 of the contributing genes and metabolites are annotated. Some of the results are shown below (see the result file for the high-definition version of the picture):

Figure3. O2PLS data integration analysis (The upper box is the positive ion mode, the lower box is the negative ion mode)

Note: The yellow triangles in the figure represent genes and the green dots represent metabolites. The horizontal and vertical coordinates of the graph indicate the scores of metabolome and transcriptome association. The distance from the origin of each point in the graph represents the weight of the gene or metabolite in the association analysis. The further away from the origin, the more the changes of the gene/metabolite affects the other grouping; the closer the distance between the two points, the more relevant the correlation between them is.
